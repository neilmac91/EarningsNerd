## The arc

Revenue (net interest income plus noninterest income) grew from $96.6 billion in FY2016 to $182 billion in FY2025 [D5][D206], a 7.3% compound rate. {comparison} Net income fell 20.0% to $29.1 billion in FY2020 [D94][F25] as $12.2 billion of COVID-19 reserve additions lifted the provision to $17.5 billion [X142][Q1]. {cause} It rose 66% to $48.3 billion in FY2021 [F26][Q2] when a $12.1 billion reserve release on a better outlook made the provision a net benefit [X143][Q3]. {cause} Higher rates then lifted net interest income 27.5% in FY2022 and 33.8% in FY2023, the latter helped by First Republic [D149][D174][Q4][Q5]. {cause}

## What drove growth

Net interest income compounded at 8.4% against 6.2% for noninterest income [F111][F112], raising its revenue share from 47.7% to 52.3% [D8][D209]. {comparison} In FY2025 revenue grew 2.8% [D226]: Markets and card balances lifted net interest income 3.1% despite deposit margin compression [D221][P28], and noninterest income rose 2.4% as a $7.9 billion Visa gain booked in FY2024 fell away [D222][P27]. {cause} On the segment basis starting FY2023 [Q6][S78], the Commercial & Investment Bank's net income rose 36.9% to $27.8 billion by FY2025, 48.7% of the total [S80][F30], while Consumer & Community Banking's fell 14.1% [S87][S89]. {comparison} Diluted EPS compounded at 13.9% against 9.7% for net income as the share count fell 23.8% [F115][F113][X83][X92]. {comparison}

## Margins and costs

The efficiency ratio was 52.4% in FY2025 [D207], against a 51.7% low in FY2024 [D331], a 59.2% high in FY2022 [D332] and a 57.2% median [D333] that reads slightly low because its FY2016–FY2017 inputs pair restated revenue with first-reported expense [D6][D15][Q7]. {comparison} The ratio rose 0.7pp in FY2025 as expense grew 4.2% and revenue 2.8% [D184][D207][D225][D226], driven mainly by compensation [P26]. {cause} The largest move was a 7.5pp fall from FY2022 to FY2024 [D135][D184], mostly in FY2023, when revenue grew 22.9% and expense 14.5%, including a $2.9 billion FDIC special assessment [D179][D178][P42]. {cause} Credit costs took 7.8% of revenue in FY2025 [D208], the most since FY2020's 14.6% [D88], as charge-offs rose $1.2 billion and reserves included $2.2 billion for the Apple Card transaction [Q8]. {cause} A lower tax rate, 21.4% in FY2025 against 28.4% in FY2016 [D203][D1], let net income compound at 9.7% against 8.6% for pretax income [F113][X59][X68]. {comparison}

## Cash conversion

Operating cash flow is not a meaningful earnings-quality measure for a bank: it swung from $107 billion in FY2022 to −$148 billion in FY2025, when higher trading assets used cash [F57][F60][P21]. {cause} Buybacks and dividends hit a window high of $48.2 billion in FY2025, 0.85x net income [D205][F30], after suspensions cut them to 0.66x in FY2020 and 0.44x in FY2022 [D85][F25][D133][F27][Q9][Q10]. {cause}

## Balance sheet and capital allocation

JPM's 16.1% return on average equity and 52.4% efficiency ratio ranked best of seven bank peers (medians 11.8% and 61.7%) [D213][D207][D338][D339][D336][D337], but credit costs sat at the 75th percentile of four peers (median 6.0%) [D208][D340][D341]. {comparison} Loans fell to 43.1% of deposits in FY2021 [D115] after pandemic inflows lifted deposits 37.2% in FY2020 [X201][X202][Q11], and were 57.3% in FY2025 [D211], when loans grew 10.9% against 6.4% for deposits [X196][X197][X206][X207]. {cause} The allowance grew a slower 5.8% [X215][X216], leaving coverage at 1.8% against 2.9% at the FY2020 peak [D212][D92]. {comparison} The standardized CET1 ratio fell to 14.6% at FY2025 from 15.7% at FY2024 [Q12][Q13]. {fact} *Analysis:* with most earnings paid out and loans outgrowing deposits, capital now paces buybacks. {judgement}

## Red flags

- **Rising credit costs (medium, three years):** the provision's share of revenue rose each year from 5.0% in FY2022 to 7.8% in FY2025 [D136][D161][D185][D208], with card charge-offs reflecting "credit normalization" and the seasoning of recent vintages [Q16]. {cause} *Analysis:* rising charge-offs [Q16][Q8] make this more than the one-off Apple Card reserve. {judgement}
- **Dropped as benign:** the FY2020 recast of pretax income and tax [R2][R3] moves both equally, leaving net income unchanged [F25]. {fact}

## What to watch

- **Credit:** in the FY2026 10-K, a provision above 7.8% of revenue [D208] or a card charge-off rate above the roughly 3.4% guided [Q14] would extend the rise. {comparison}
- **Net interest income:** a FY2026 10-K figure below the "approximately $103 billion" management expects [Q15] would signal lower rates outrunning balance growth. {comparison}
- **Efficiency:** a FY2026 ratio above the 57.2% median [D333] would undo the FY2022–FY2024 gains; one below 51.7% [D331] would extend them. {comparison}
