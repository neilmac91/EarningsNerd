Analysis B

The arc

Revenue compounded at 12.8% a year, from $4.86 billion in FY2016 to $14.3 billion in FY2025 [1][2][3]. FY2021 was the step change: revenue rose 89.1% to $16.1 billion, with REGEN-COV U.S. sales of $5.83 billion adding 66.4pp under a September 2021 U.S. government supply agreement [4][5][6][7][8]. Revenue then fell 24.3% to $12.2 billion in FY2022 because REGEN-COV U.S. sales, $0 that year, did not recur [9][10][11][12]. Growth has slowed since: the FY2022 to FY2025 revenue CAGR is 5.6%, and FY2025 growth was 1.0% [13][14].

What drove growth

In FY2025 net product sales fell 17.3%, subtracting 9.3pp from growth, while collaboration revenue rose 21.0%, adding 9.0pp [15][16][17][18]. U.S. EYLEA sales fell 42.4% to $2.75 billion, a 14.2pp drag, on lower volumes, competition, compounded bevacizumab, the shift to EYLEA HD and a lower net price [19][20][21][22]. Regeneron's share of Sanofi antibody profits rose 33.6% to $5.24 billion, 36.5% of revenue, driven mainly by higher Dupixent sales [23][24][25][26]. Revenue growth of 1.0% ran 4.4pp below operating-cost growth in FY2025, and operating income fell 10.3% [14][27][28]. Diluted EPS grew 8.2% against net income growth of 2.1%, because the diluted share count fell 5.6% [29][30][31].

Margins and costs

Operating margin of 24.9% in FY2025 is the window low, against a median of 34.6% and a high of 55.7% [32][33][34][35]. In FY2025 R&D cost 4.7pp of margin as R&D grew 14.0%, while SG&A added 2.0pp [36][37][38]. SG&A fell mainly on lower charitable contributions to Good Days, a patient assistance organization [38][39]. The largest move was FY2022, down 16.7pp, with R&D costing 11.4pp and SG&A 6.0pp, the latter on higher headcount and Libtayo commercialization costs [40][41][42][43]. Net margin of 31.4% sits above operating margin of 24.9%, with other income of $1.65 billion in FY2025 [44][32][45].

Cash conversion

FY2025 operating cash flow of $4.98 billion was 1.11x net income, while free cash flow of $4.08 billion was 0.91x [46][47][48][49]. Free cash flow margin of 28.4% is above the window median of 26.4% [50][51]. Capex of $898 million was 6.3% of revenue and 1.65x D&A, after growing 18.9% [52][53][54][55]. Free cash flow to net income of 0.91x ranks in the 29th percentile of seven peers, whose median is 1.11x [49][56][57].

Balance sheet and capital allocation

Net cash reached $16.9 billion in FY2025, up from $15.9 billion, equal to 117.7% of revenue [58][59][60]. That ranks in the 100th percentile of eight biopharma peers, whose median net cash is -50.7% of revenue [61][62]. The current ratio of 4.13x compares with its own window median of 3.97x [63][64]. Buybacks of $3.44 billion and dividends of $370 million took 93.3% of free cash flow, against 71.0% in FY2024 [65][66][67][68]. The diluted share count fell 5.6% to 108.6 million shares [31][69].

Red flags

  • Operating margin fell four years in a row, 30.7pp in total, from 55.7% to 24.9% [70][71][32].
  • R&D rose from 18.1% to 40.8% of revenue, and the FY2023 increase was partly due to the Sanofi amendments [72][73][74][75].
  • U.S. EYLEA sales have fallen three years running, by 8.7%, 16.7% and 42.4%, leaving 19.2% of revenue against 63.0% in FY2017 [76][77][20][78][79].
  • Other income of $1.65 billion in FY2025 compares with $789 million in FY2024, and the cited filings do not explain the rise [45][80].
  • Data quality: the table's FY2018 revenue of $5.15 billion and FY2019 revenue of $6.56 billion are recasts of $6.71 billion and $7.86 billion first reported [81][82][83][84][85].
  • Analysis: I draw no conclusion from growth or margin comparisons across FY2018 and FY2019, and the CAGRs use unaffected endpoints [3].

What to watch

  • Analysis: Operating margin below the 24.9% window low would confirm the compression trend, and a move above it would reverse it; see the FY2026 10-K [33][32].
  • Analysis: U.S. EYLEA HD and EYLEA sales below the FY2025 level of $4.38 billion would confirm the erosion, and sales above it would reverse it; see the FY2026 10-K [86].
  • Analysis: Buybacks plus dividends above the FY2025 level of 93.3% of free cash flow would show returns outrunning cash generation; see the FY2026 10-K [67].

The arc

Net interest income compounded at 8.4% a year from FY2016 to FY2025, while noninterest income compounded at 6.2% [1][2]. Net income compounded at 9.7% and diluted EPS at 13.9% over the same window, so per-share growth outpaced earnings growth [3][4]. Net interest income fell to $52.3 billion in FY2021, then rose 27.5% in FY2022 and 33.8% in FY2023 [5][6][7]. The cited passages explain this only for the Corporate segment, where net revenue rose to $8.0 billion on higher net interest income due to higher rates [8]. Net income fell 20.0% in FY2020 and rose 65.8% in FY2021, as the provision swung from $17.5 billion to a $9.3 billion release [9][10][11][12].

What drove growth

Commercial And Investment Bank noninterest income reached $53.8 billion in FY2025, up from $48.3 billion in FY2024 [13][14]. Noninterest revenue rose 2.4% to $87.0 billion in FY2025 on higher Markets revenue, largely offset by the absence of the $7.9 billion Visa share gain recorded in 2024 [15][16][17]. Net interest income grew 3.1% to $95.4 billion, driven by Markets net interest income and Card Services revolving balances, largely offset by deposit margin compression [18][19][20]. Asia-Pacific and EMEA each added 1.2pp to FY2025 revenue growth, against 0.2pp from North America [21][22][23]. Noninterest expense grew 4.2% in FY2025 against revenue growth of 2.8%, so costs outran revenue [24][25].

Margins and costs

The FY2025 efficiency ratio of 52.4% sits near the window low of 51.7% and well below the window median of 57.2% [26][27][28]. That ratio is below the banks peer median of 61.7%, the lowest among the seven peers [26][29][30]. Noninterest expense reached $95.6 billion, driven by higher compensation, including revenue-related pay and headcount growth [31][32]. The credit-loss provision rose to 7.8% of revenue from 6.0%, including a $2.2 billion allowance addition tied to the Apple Card transaction [33][34][35]. Analysis: Rising provisions and costs explain why net income fell 2.6% despite higher revenue [36][25].

Cash conversion

Operating cash flow is not a meaningful earnings-quality measure for a bank, and was an outflow of $147.8 billion in FY2025 [37]. Cash used in operations reflected higher trading assets, securities borrowed and loans held-for-sale, which are balance-sheet activity rather than weaker earnings [37][38]. Capital return is the better test: buybacks plus dividends were $48.2 billion in FY2025 against net income of $57.0 billion [39][40].

Balance sheet and capital allocation

Shareholders' equity rose 5.1% to $362 billion in FY2025, while loans reached 57.3% of deposits from 55.0% [41][42][43][44]. Cash and equivalents fell to $343 billion from $469 billion, driven by Markets activities, higher securities and higher loans [45][46][47]. Return on average equity of 16.1% ranks at the 100th percentile of seven banks peers, against a peer median of 11.8% [48][49][50]. Buybacks rose to $31.6 billion from $18.8 billion, and the diluted share count fell 3.4% [51][52][53].

Red flags

  • Credit costs are rising: the provision reached 7.8% of revenue in FY2025, the highest since FY2020's 14.6% and the 75th percentile of four peers [33][54][55].
  • The peer median is 6.0%, so JPMorgan provisions more than most of the group [56].
  • Criticized wholesale exposure rose to $48.5 billion from $44.7 billion, primarily on downgrades and new lending commitments [57].
  • Nonperforming exposure rose $478 million, driven by downgrades in Technology, Media & Telecommunications, Oil & Gas and Utilities [58].
  • Data quality: FY2021 to FY2025 net income in the table comes from the proxy and is rounded, for example $57,000 million against the 10-K's $57,048 million in FY2025 [59][40][60].

  • An efficiency ratio above the FY2025 level of 52.4% would confirm cost growth outrunning revenue, while a return toward the 51.7% window low would reverse it, in the FY2026 10-K [26][27].
  • A provision above 7.8% of revenue would confirm worsening credit costs, while a fall toward the 6.0% peer median would reverse it, in the FY2026 10-K [33][56].
  • Net interest income excluding Markets below the FY2025 level of $92.6 billion, which was flat on the prior year, would confirm deposit margin compression, in the FY2026 10-K [20].

Analysis A

The arc

Revenue compounded at 10.0% a year, from $129 billion in FY2017 to $303 billion in FY2026 [1][2][3]. Operating income grew faster at 12.3% a year [4], lifting operating margin from 3.2% in FY2017 to 3.9% in FY2026 [5][6]. FY2021 revenue grew 17.5% [7], as net sales rose 18% on a 16% comparable sales increase, with strong non-foods and e-commerce [8][9]. FY2022 revenue grew 15.8% [10], attributed to a 14% comparable sales increase, new warehouses and higher inflation than in earlier years [11].

What drove growth

Warehouse ancillary and other businesses contributed +3.7pp of FY2026 growth [12], against +0.5pp in FY2025 [13], as sales rose 19.9% [14]. The 10-K says these businesses grew faster than core merchandise categories, but it gives no separate cause for the FY2026 jump beyond sales mix [15]. Food and Sundries remains the largest category at 38.5% of FY2026 revenue and added +2.6pp to growth [16][17]. FY2026 net sales rose 10%, driven by comparable sales up 8% (average ticket up 5%, frequency up 3%) and 25 net new warehouses [18][19]. Operating income grew 12.5% against revenue growth of 10.1% [20][19], an incremental operating margin of 4.7% [21]. Diluted EPS grew 14.0% [22], just above net income growth of 13.9% [23], because the diluted share count changed by only -0.1% [24].

Margins and costs

Operating margin of 3.9% in FY2026 equals the window high, against a 3.4% median and 3.1% low [6][25][26][27]. That margin ranks in the 17th percentile of six retail peers, whose median is 5.0% [28][29][30]. In FY2026 cost of revenue moved operating margin by -0.0pp and SG&A by +0.1pp [31][32]; SG&A fell 10 basis points as a share of sales [33]. Gross margin percentage fell three basis points in FY2026 as faster growth in gasoline and pharmacy shifted sales mix [15][31]. The window's largest margin rise was +0.3pp in FY2024 [34]; cost of revenue added 0.4pp [35] as charter shipping charges did not recur [36].

Cash conversion

FY2026 operating cash flow of $15.8 billion was 1.71x net income, and free cash flow of $9.38 billion was 1.02x [37][38][39][40]. Free cash flow conversion of 1.02x compares with a window median of 0.99x, and the 3.1% free cash flow margin with a 2.8% median [40][41][42][43]. Operating cash flow rose from $13,335 million to $15,817 million on higher operating income and faster inventory turns with better supplier terms [44][45][37]. Capex reached 2.1% of revenue and 2.41x depreciation, up from 1.83x in FY2017 [46][47][48].

Balance sheet and capital allocation

Net cash rose to $15.1 billion in FY2026 from $9.50 billion in FY2025 [49][50]. Net cash is 5.0% of revenue, the 100th percentile of six retail peers, whose median is -4.4% [51][52][53]. Current debt rose to $2,248 million from $75 million [54][55], while cash and equivalents of $20.2 billion cover it [56]. Buybacks plus dividends were $3,306 million, or 35.2% of free cash flow, with buybacks at $848 million [57][58][59]. The diluted share count was 444.4 million against 440.9 million in FY2017, so repurchases only offset issuance [60][61].

Analysis A

The arc

Revenue compounded at 24.2% a year, from $1.70 billion in FY2016 to $12.0 billion in FY2025 [1][2][3]. Revenue grew 49.1% in FY2020 [4], driven by TRIKAFTA's US launch in the fourth quarter of 2019 and KAFTRIO's EU launch in the third quarter of 2020 [5]. Operating margin fell from 38.8% in FY2023 to -2.1% in FY2024, a 40.9pp drop, as acquired in-process R&D of $4.6 billion included $4.4 billion for Alpine [6][7][8][9]. Growth has since slowed, with revenue up 8.9% in FY2025 [10] against a 3-year CAGR of 10.4% [11].

What drove growth

TRIKAFTA/KAFTRIO reached 85.9% of revenue in FY2025, up from 62.3% in FY2020 [12][13]. It added 29.5pp to growth in FY2021 and 26.3pp in FY2022 but only 0.7pp in FY2025 [14][15][16]. ALYFTREK, with $837.8 million of FY2025 sales, added 7.6pp to growth, more than TRIKAFTA/KAFTRIO's 0.7pp [17][18][16]. Profit has lagged revenue: the 3-year operating income CAGR was -1.0% against revenue's 10.4% [19][11]. Diluted shares were 258.0 million in FY2025, unchanged from the prior year, so EPS growth tracked net income [20][21].

Margins and costs

FY2025 operating margin of 34.8% sits above the 31.8% window median but below the 48.2% high and far above the -2.1% low [22][23][24][25]. Margin rose 36.9pp in FY2025 [26], almost all from the residual line at +37.7pp [27]. The residual reflects the prior-year charge of $4.6 billion of acquired in-process R&D, including $4.4 billion for Alpine, not repeating [9][27]. SG&A cost 1.3pp of margin, rising to 14.6% of revenue from 13.3%, and the FY2024 10-K guided to higher 2025 spend for launches [28][29][30][31]. Cost of sales was 13.8% of revenue in FY2025 [32], with the filing citing a lower overall royalty rate for CF medicines [33].

Cash conversion

Operating cash flow of $3.63 billion was 0.92x net income, and free cash flow was 0.81x, against a window median of 1.07x [34][35][36][37]. The filing attributes operating cash flow primarily to income from operations of $4.17 billion [38][39]. Free cash flow margin was 26.6% against a 32.8% median, and capex was 3.6% of revenue and 2.09x D&A [40][41][42][43].

Balance sheet and capital allocation

Net cash rose to $12.3 billion from $11.2 billion [44][45], and working capital rose to $7.34 billion from $6.03 billion on cash from product revenue [46][47][48]. Net cash equals 102.7% of revenue, the 88th percentile among 8 biopharma peers, whose median is -50.7% [49][50][51]. Buybacks were $2.02 billion in FY2025, 63.2% of free cash flow, up from $1.18 billion in FY2024 [52][53][54]. Return on average equity of 22.5% is the 38th percentile of 8 peers, below their 38.7% median [55][56][57].
