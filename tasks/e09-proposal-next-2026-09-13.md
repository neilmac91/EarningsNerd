## Capacity arithmetic — assumptions, not headroom

For the previously observed single serving revision, assuming two active instances, one process per instance and one engine per process, maximum configured connection demand is `2 × 1 × (12 + 8) = 40`. Request concurrency gives `2 × 40 = 80` configured request slots, not 80 simultaneous generations or database connections. If there are P processes per instance, the connection expression becomes `40 × P`. Rollout/old-revision overlap, jobs and other services add separately.

General planning expression: `sum(service instances × processes × engines × (pool + overflow)) + sum(overlapping job tasks × processes × engines × (pool + overflow)) + other database clients`. Use observed effective values in each term. Source pins jobs at 3+2 for the deployment paths that update them; if confirmed effective, one process/engine per active task contributes at most five configured connections. Eight listed jobs do **not** justify adding forty: actual simultaneous tasks, overlaps and runtime overrides are unknown. The service-level maximum 20 and revision maximum 2 must not be multiplied into forty instances; reconcile their effective interaction and revision overlap instead.

Database reserve for administration, migrations and other clients must be chosen against actual limits/peaks; no numeric reserve or sizing change is justified here. A connection-capacity sum is an upper-bound planning model, not proof of actual usage or healthy coordination latency. SEC rate arithmetic likewise needs actual attempts and overlap; dividing a nominal cap by eight jobs is not a safe fleet policy.
